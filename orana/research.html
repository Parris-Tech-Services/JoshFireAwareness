01 / Findings

What we can establish.

1948

Burrabadine's beginnings

The NSW RFS celebrated the brigade's 75th anniversary in August 2023, placing its founding around 1948. The RFS describes an enduring tradition of community, fire response and aviation support from Dubbo.

1.28m

Hectares in the Orana fire-planning area

The Orana bushfire management area covers Dubbo Regional and Narromine Shire. The public risk planning data describes approximately 61% grassland and 37% bushland, highlighting the importance of grassfire and farm preparedness.

Official Orana bushfire risk ↗
2026

Fleet safety and compliance work

Statewide RFS fleet assessments have identified weight and axle-load compliance issues, including Category 9 light appliances. NSW procurement includes fleet inspections and upgrade tenders. These are statewide programs; no individual local vehicle's status is established here.

Official NSW fleet tender ↗
15%

Standard meeting quorum

The published model brigade constitution sets a standard general-meeting quorum of 15% of ordinary members unless an approved brigade rule increases it. Probationary, ordinary and associate membership are different categories.

Brigade constitution ↗
2026–31

The service's next five years

The new strategic direction recognises challenges around volunteer sustainability, rising equipment and technology costs, family/work availability, fatigue, training, cybersecurity and community trust.

2023

Dubbo's shared training facility

The Bob Conran Training Facility, opened at the Dubbo airport emergency precinct in July 2023, was announced as available to Orana's 60 brigades for advanced village-firefighting and breathing-apparatus capabilities.

02 / How it fits together

One fire service.
Many forms of contribution.

Rural brigades respond to fires and other emergencies, but also support preparation, education, community activities and recovery. Brigades have elected field and administrative officers; Orana Fire Control Centre supports and coordinates local responses. Roles, qualifications and incident mobilisation are governed by RFS rules and current directions, not by a website.

Local brigade finances, the central NSW RFS agency budget, and the independent RFS & Brigades Donations Fund should be understood separately. A budget statement or funding concern alone does not show whether a particular brigade can operate.
